# Loan_Default_Risk_Prediction_With_ML

A credit default risk classification project comparing four classification models. It includes EDA, leakage-safe preprocessing, class imbalance handling, Grid Search optimization, and evaluation with ROC-AUC, Gini, and K-S. The final XGBoost model supports a credit origination strategy based on risk deciles.

## Project Objective

The main objective of this project is to transform the results of a predictive credit risk model into a business-oriented value proposition by segmenting customers according to their estimated probability of default.

As a secondary objective, four supervised learning classification models are developed and compared using metrics suitable for evaluating discriminatory power in an imbalanced classification problem.

## Dataset

The dataset contains 14,067 credit applications, including qualitative and quantitative variables related to different aspects of customer credit profiles and behavior. These variables can be conceptually grouped into the following categories:

- Risk score
- Payment capacity
- Risk alerts
- Indebtedness
- Credit history
- Payment behavior
- Sector exposure
- Financial profile
- Demographic profile

The target variable *"ind_malo"* identifies whether a customer experienced a Default or Paid on Time. The target distribution is approximately 21% Default and 79% Paid on Time, an imbalance that is taken into account during model training and evaluation.

## Methodology

The following steps were carried out to train and compare the four models.

- EDA: Initial exploration of the dataset to understand its structure, categorical variable cardinality, numerical variable distributions and ranges, missing values, and target variable behavior.

- Class imbalance handling: The target variable contains 11,097 Paid on Time cases and 2,970 Default cases. To compensate for this imbalance, class-weighting mechanisms were applied during training, including `class_weight = "balanced"` and `scale_pos_weight`.

- Data split: Training `70%` / Validation `15%` / Test `15%`, preserving the original target distribution through stratification.

- Missing value imputation: Missing values were replaced using the median calculated exclusively from the training set, reducing the impact of extreme values and preventing data leakage.

- Numerical feature scaling: Non-normalized numerical variables were transformed using parameters learned exclusively from the training set.

- One-Hot Encoding of categorical variables: Nominal categorical variables were transformed using One-Hot Encoding, fitting the encoder exclusively on the training data.

## Models

Four supervised classification models (`Logistic Regression`, `XGBoost`, `Random Forest Classifier`, and `Gradient Boosting`) were trained and compared to identify the approach with the strongest risk discrimination capability.

`Fine-tuning` with `Grid Search` was used to optimize the hyperparameters of the four models, using `ROC-AUC` as the selection metric.

The models were evaluated using `ROC-AUC`, `Gini Coefficient`, and `K-S (*Kolmogorov-Smirnov*)`, metrics suitable for measuring ranking ability and separation between customers who paid on time and those who defaulted.

## Model Performance

After the optimization process using `Grid Search`, the four models were evaluated on the validation set. `XGBoost` achieved the best overall performance, with a `ROC-AUC` of 0.7059, a Gini of 0.4117, and a K-S of 0.3067, followed closely by Gradient Boosting.

## Business Strategy: Proposal

Based on the default probabilities generated by the final  `XGBoost ` model, customers were grouped into risk deciles, where  `decile 1 ` represents the lowest estimated risk and  `decile 10 ` the highest.

Based on this segmentation, three groups were proposed to support the credit origination strategy:

Low Risk —  `Deciles 1 to 4 `: Customers with the lowest observed default probabilities, considered suitable for a standard credit approval process.

Medium Risk —  `Deciles 5 to 7 `: Customers who may require additional validation, more conservative credit limits, or stricter lending conditions.

High Risk —  `Deciles 8 to 10 `: Customers subject to stricter approval criteria or possible rejection depending on the institution's risk appetite.
